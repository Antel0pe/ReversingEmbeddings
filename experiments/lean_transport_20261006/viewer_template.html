How lean changes with the starting one

A lean direction is a signed pattern of pixel changes. The pattern moves with the stroke’s two edges. Use width first, then try a different starting shape to test joint changes.

Degrees; positive tilts the top rightward

The compact rule

Keep two continuous edges, then work out which pixels those edges affect. Width moves them apart. Center shifts move them together. Height changes the active rows. Lean shears the edges, with the top moving farther than the middle.

At subrow height y: left edge = horizontal center + tan(lean in radians) × (vertical center − y) − width/2 right edge = horizontal center + tan(lean in radians) × (vertical center − y) + width/2 lean change per degree in a pixel = average of vertical coverage × (vertical center − y) × (π/180) / cos(lean in radians)² × [right edge inside that pixel − left edge inside that pixel]
Why separately adding the pixel patterns fails

The separate arrows can put change into yesterday’s edge pixels. Combining the continuous edge positions first places it at the new edges. Even perfect single-axis arrows do not supply this relocation through addition or averaging.

What was learned, supplied, and tested

The edge formula comes from the known renderer. A second experiment calibrated continuous geometry transformations from 95 single-axis images and one reference image, with no joint training examples. It supplies image-to-geometry recovery, tan(lean), and the edge-to-pixel formula. Its learned coefficients are essentially the identity in physical coordinates.

Across 1,281 held-out random, lattice, and near-boundary states, those calibrated transformations predicted finite lean moves with maximum pixel error 1.05 × 10⁻⁷. Using known settings gave 5.95 × 10⁻⁸. Six complete paths from −10° to 35° also passed, by updating the tangent at every edge crossing.

The universal formula applies to this controlled family. The controls on this page select precomputed samples; they do not display the full continuous possibility space. Source images, arrows, and endpoint predictions were computed in Python.

Coverage is ink area: 0 means no ink, 1 means full ink. Every canvas contains all 28 × 28 pixels; row increases downward and column increases rightward. Signed patterns retain magnitude. At exact edge crossings, the direction view shows the average of the two one-sided limits; a two-sided derivative may not exist. The movement view uses the appropriate one-sided initial arrow.
