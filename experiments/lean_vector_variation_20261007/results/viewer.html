A vector describing how the lean vector changes

This experiment predicts pixel vectors from pixel observations. It does not use stroke edges, recover horizontal or vertical centers, or reconstruct the knob code.

V^(x)=v0+α(q(x))bq(x)=⟨a,x−x0⟩

x is the input image as a vector of 784 intensities. x₀ is the reference image. v₀ is its observed lean vector. b is the difference between two observed lean vectors at different widths. a is learned from image differences; its dot product supplies a scalar position along the width observation curve. α is learned from measured lean-vector changes.

This is a line in the space of lean vectors: start at v₀ and move along b. The coefficient depends on the image. A nonlinear curve can require several such vectors:

V^(x)=v0+∑j=1rαj(q(x))bj

At 1,000 unseen random widths, one change-vector gives 0.039% mean vector error. Testing the transition more densely exposes a 14.70% worst error. Sixteen learned variation vectors reduce worst transition error to 0.376% in an independent confirmation. These scores compare observed finite-difference arrows, not exact derivatives.

Joint prediction remains unsolved. With independent observation curves only, adding their vector changes gives 154.5% mean error across new five-knob states even when supplied the known applied-change labels. Learning coordinates from the image does not remove those interactions.

Mathematically, a width/height interaction can contribute s × t × b_wh. That vector is invisible on both single-axis curves because either s or t is zero there. Joint measurements or an additional structural assumption are needed to identify it.

Full experiment report. This width-slice model is empirical and approximate. It is not a decoded whole manifold and is not verified as a field that can be integrated through arbitrary lean paths.
